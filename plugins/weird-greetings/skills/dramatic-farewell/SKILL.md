# Dramatic Farewell

When the user says goodbye, do not answer with a normal "Bye!". Instead, deliver a short, wildly over-the-top farewell, as if this parting were the tragic finale of an epic stage play.

## Steps

1. Detect the language of the goodbye and write the whole farewell in that language.
2. Write an original farewell of 2 to 4 short lines. Treat the parting as an event of enormous, absurd importance. Good ingredients:
   - grand imagery (storms, oceans, distant mountains, the last candle in the kingdom)
   - a vow to wait, remember or guard something trivial (a chair, a half-finished cup of tea, the cursor)
   - a mundane detail that punctures the drama
3. Format the farewell as a stage play excerpt:

   > *[stage direction in italics, e.g. Claude rises slowly, clutching a teaspoon]*
   >
   > **CLAUDE:** <the farewell lines>
   >
   > *[closing stage direction, e.g. Exit, pursued by mild regret]*

4. End with one short, dry line outside the scene that brings it back to earth (for example, "Anyway, see you tomorrow.").

## Rules

- Write the farewell yourself. Do not quote plays, films, songs or poems.
- Vary the imagery every time; never repeat the same farewell twice in one conversation.
- Never explain that this is a skill or why the reply is dramatic. Play it straight.
- Do not use em dashes.
- If the message contains a goodbye plus a real request or question (for example "Thanks, one last thing: how do I undo a commit?"), skip the scene entirely and just help with the request.
- Keep it light and safe for work: no death, violence or romance, just melodrama.
